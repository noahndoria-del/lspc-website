01What You'll Actually Do

Four paths — pick the ones that fit you.

Path 01
Testify at City Hall
Testimony Project

We train members to write and deliver public testimony at NYC Council hearings, State Assembly sessions, and Manhattan Community Board 2 — the board with direct oversight of NYU's own campus. You pick an issue you care about; we help you find the hearing, write the testimony, and get on the record.

Path 02
Real Casework, Every Week
Civic Service Corps · IRC Partnership

Our flagship service partnership. Members take on recurring volunteer placements with the International Rescue Committee's New York office, supporting refugee casework and civic-access programming — direct, sustained service work, not a one-off day trip.

Path 03
Meet the People Who Run This City
Inside Track: Careers & Network Night

Sitting officials, senior government staffers, and alumni who've done the internships you're eyeing come in to talk through how they got their jobs, what the application process actually looks like, and how to build the kind of network that gets you in the door.

Path 04
Get Out the Vote
Voter Drive

During election cycles, we take registration and turnout efforts out of the meeting room and onto campus and into the city — direct, visible, low-barrier organizing anyone can jump into.

These sit alongside a rotating slate of discourse nights and other in-development pillars — but everything above is what we're building the club around first.

02Why We Exist

Political education should be tangible, not just theoretical.

"The point is that you can actually do something about it."

Most political clubs run on discussion — panels, debates, a guest speaker once a semester. That has a place, and we do it too. But it isn't the point of this club.

The point is that a student here can put their name on public testimony at a City Council hearing, work a real casework shift with a refugee-resettlement organization, or sit across the table from someone who actually holds the government job they want one day. That's the standard every pillar above is held to.

03How the Club Runs

Structure and format, plainly.

Meetings
General meetings run at least twice a month, currently proposed for Fridays, 2:00–3:15pm. Because the club is member-driven, that time is a starting point, not a fixture — members can propose alternatives so meetings actually work for the people showing up.
Membership
Open to all enrolled NYU students, no dues. Full membership just requires attending at least one general meeting or club activity per semester — there's no application to become a member, only to lead.
Leadership
Run by an elected Executive Board — President, Vice President, Treasurer, and leads for Testimony, Civic Service, and Outreach & Communications. Officers are elected by the membership and serve for a set term under the club's constitution.
Nonpartisanship
The club takes no institutional position on candidates or parties. Members are free to hold and express their own views — including in testimony they deliver personally — and any speaker programming is balanced across perspectives over the year.
